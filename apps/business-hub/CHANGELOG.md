# Business Hub: what changed

The Business Hub is the part of the Smart Retail AI Ecosystem that runs any kind of business (see `README.md`). Newest first. (The older Windows programs keep their own list in the top-level `CHANGELOG.md`.)

## Unreleased

### New versions of the program, through the main PC
- **The main PC looks, about once a day, for a newer version** (when this copy was built to, and the owner has not switched it off): *Settings, Updates* says where things stand, and the first screen reminds the owner when a version is ready. It sends only the program's name and version and a time stamp: nothing about the shop.
- **A new version is trusted only when the makers' own release made it.** GitHub signs a statement that this repository's release workflow, started by a version tag, made this very file; it is checked before anything is downloaded, and the file's size and fingerprint are checked when it is kept and again when the owner approves. Nobody has to keep an update-signing key.
- **Nothing is installed until the owner says so.** *Approve, and copy the shop first* copies the shop (to the second place when there is one; if the copy cannot be made, nothing is approved) and then names the checked file and the last step in plain words. *Not now* stops the reminder for that version.
- **Not yet:** the install itself is the owner's last step (a Windows service cannot install a program or ask the owner); a new look for a client; a quiet hour. The online folder is set up by the owner (`docs/UPDATES.md`).
- **Found and fixed on the way:** the Help button's support file printed the licence state from a name the shipped program hides (it showed a letter); it is now written in plain words. And a release started by pushing a tag was always built as version 1.0.0; it now carries the tag's version.

### The Hub itself now keeps reports, registers, books and exports to the people who may see them
- **Reading the shop's numbers is checked inside the Hub, as changing them already was.** The reports, the tax registers and return lists, the books (trial balance, profit, position) and the spreadsheet files (*/export/...*) now refuse a person whose role does not allow reports. A new screen, a counter PC or an assistant cannot see more than the role shows. The first screen's total for the day stays for everyone who sells; stock value for those who manage stock; what a customer or supplier owes for the people who work with accounts, bills, orders, buying and bookings. The *Owed* total on the first screen is now left out for a person who may not read reports.
- Found by the new tests on the way: the day's total on the first screen would have been refused to a cashier by the first version of this change; fixed before it went out.
- Not yet: the event sources, and keeping one store's data from another's when several stores exist.

### Help: a support file you read before you send it
- **The owner's menu has a new entry, *Help*.** Say what went wrong in your own words and press *Make the support file*. The whole file is shown on the screen, and *Save it as a file* keeps exactly that text. **The program sends nothing**: you decide whether to send it, and to whom (the supplier's contact shows when the licence or your own settings give one).
- **What is in it:** the program's version and the PC it runs on, how the licence stands (number, trial or paid, end date, parts), whether the shop's file is sound and the books add up, the nightly copies (last good one, last failed one), counter PCs, which AI helpers are on and how the waiting lines stand, how often a few important things happened in the last 7 days (numbers only), and the last warnings and errors the program noticed since it started. **What is not:** sales, customers, staff names, amounts, passwords or keys, the shop's name, and folders on your PC. Folders, e-mail addresses, telephone and long numbers, card numbers, network addresses, passwords and keys written as `name=value`, and every name or address the shop keeps for a customer, supplier or member of staff are taken out of whatever is left (including what you typed). Making a file is written in the activity list.
- Not yet: the client's page in the Studio that receives it; Help for roles other than the owner; the older Windows programs.

### What leaves the shop for an AI service, field by field
- **A feature can no longer send "some text" to an AI service.** It names one of a short fixed list of reasons and fills that reason's fields; the program builds the text itself and leaves out anything else. A value that is a card number, an e-mail address or telephone number, a link, a name or address the shop keeps for a customer, supplier or member of staff, an instruction to the service, or simply the wrong shape stops the whole request before anything is sent. The owner's existing rules still apply on top (card and biometric data never leave this computer; an online service needs the online switch and permission for that kind of data and that feature).
- **Settings, AI helpers, *What was sent* lists every such request**, also the refused ones: the reason, the service and where it runs, which pieces went (their names and kinds, never what they said), which supplied pieces were left out, the date of the permission relied on, the cost and the result. A permission taken back stops the next request; a permission given again is a new date.
- **Fixed on the way:** a card number written in groups with other digits beside it ("4111 1111 1111 1111 51") was not recognised as a card number; it is now. The guide is `docs/WHAT-LEAVES-THE-SHOP.md`. Database step 19. No screen uses a reason yet.

### Suggested actions: the program prepares, a person approves
- **A warning can turn into a request for an order, and nothing is ordered until a person who may buy says yes.** Switch on *Suggested actions* (Settings, AI helpers). On a *Running low* warning, *Ask for approval* puts a request on the Buying page under *Waiting for approval*, described in plain words ("Draft an order to National Foods for 20 kg of Toor dal, … in all"). A manager or the owner presses *Approve and draft the order* (or *Decline*), and only then is an ordinary draft order made, which is received and paid for as always. Nothing is bought, paid or sent to the supplier by asking or approving.
- **The safeguards are the point.** Only people who work with stock can ask and only people who may buy can approve, judged at the moment they act; a request runs out after 48 hours; an approval is refused if the supplier is different or a cost price has moved by more than a tenth since it was asked for; pressing twice, or two people approving at once, makes one order; an order that repeats one still open to the same supplier is refused; every step is written down with who took it and why. The guide is `docs/SUGGESTED-ACTIONS.md`. Database step 18 (two new tables, with their way back).
- Not yet: other kinds of request (adjusting stock, a customer payment, a payment reminder, a discount), a second approver above a limit, an assistant that asks.

### Running low: a warning before the shelf is empty
- **The Buying page can tell you which goods will run out before a new delivery could arrive.** Switch on *Stock forecasts* (Settings, AI helpers), then under *Buying → Delivery times* say, for each thing you keep in stock, who supplies it, how many days they take to deliver, how many spare days you want, the pack it comes in and the least they send. *Running low* then looks at how fast each thing has really been selling (returns and cancelled bills count against it) and compares how many days what is on the shelf lasts with the days the supplier needs plus your spare days. It is plain arithmetic on your own figures on this computer; no AI service is used.
- **It shows its reasons.** Each warning says, in words, what is on the shelf, what sold, how many days it lasts, what the supplier needs and how much would cover the next days, less what is already on order; *Why might this be wrong?* lists the ways the figure could mislead and the bills it rests on. A suggestion is never an order: *Start an order* only fills in the order form, and a person places it.
- **Goods with no delivery time, goods that are new and goods that are not selling are counted and said so, never guessed.** *Set aside* keeps a warning quiet for the days you choose (the record is kept). The days of selling looked at, the days an order should cover beyond delivery and the quiet days are the owner's settings. It is checked once a day and when you press *Check now*. Database step 17 (four new tables, with their way back).
- Also: the shop's upkeep now writes the business-event messages in several batches in one go (a busy day left more than one batch).

### The business map shows each person only what their screens show them
- **A person sees a kind of thing in the business map only if the shop's own screens already show it to them.** A cashier sees products, customers, bills and payments, but not the people who work here, the owner's projects or the cameras; a kitchen screen sees menu items, tables and places, and no money or customers; a kind the owner adds is shown by what it holds (public and internal to everyone who works here, confidential to those who see the reports, personal to those who see the customer list; card, biometric, picture and sound kinds to nobody).
- **Hidden looks the same as missing.** Asking for something you may not see gives the same answer as asking for something that is not there, and a connection is left out when the thing at its other end is hidden (a cashier sees who a bill was for and what was on it, not which member of staff handled it). The check is made before anything is returned, so the same rule will hold for an assistant. Each thing says when it was read and whether it is a shop record (read live) or kept only in the map; worked-out connections say so too.
- A read with nobody named is refused in the shop as people use it, like a command. *Check the map* is for the owner. Not yet: limits by location (a shop has one site today).

### Business events never get lost (the outbox)
- **What happens in the shop is left as a message in the same step that makes it.** A sale, a payment or refund, a return, a void, a purchase and a change of stock each leave one message (figures and numbers only: never a name, a note, a card detail or what was typed with a payment), written in the very transaction of the sale, so the sale and its message exist together or not at all. Only while the *business events* switch (Settings, AI) is on and the licence has it; with it off nothing is written and the till is unchanged.
- **A helper delivers the messages to the event history, once each.** Every ten minutes and when the shop opens, also after the program was stopped half-way; two helpers at the same moment, or a message sent twice, still make one event. A message that cannot be delivered is tried again later and later (at most an hour apart), set aside after eight tries, and shown on the *Business events* page with a *Try again* button. Nothing waits on it: the till never does.
- Database step 16 (two new tables, with their way back). Not yet: a second helper that reads these messages (the low-stock rule), a screen to deliver a period again (the code is there).

### When a licence ends (decision 15)
- **A paid licence that has ended keeps working and says so; a trial stops.** What happens at the end date is written inside the signed licence (never a setting on the PC): the Licence Studio signs "keep working with a banner" for a paid licence and "stop" for a trial. The Hub shows a banner across every screen: for a trial, when it ends; for a paid licence that has ended, that it ended and the shop keeps working; for a PC that could not check in, how many days it still works. Staff can still switch any licence off at any time.
- Not yet: the same banner in the older Windows programs, and "read-only, export your data" for a stopped trial.

### What stock costs (average cost, decision 36)
- **Every item has one cost: the average of what was paid, worked out again at each purchase.** A purchase brings its goods in at what its lines cost without tax and after discounts; a sale takes its goods off at the average cost of that moment (the last unit takes whatever is left, so nothing stays behind); goods a customer brings back return at the cost they left at, and the returns of one bill add up to exactly what it cost; a cancelled sale or purchase undoes its value; a count or damage is valued at the average, a delivery with no order at the last cost price. Database step 15, with its way back.
- **The books count profit from what was sold.** Bought goods sit in *Stock on the shelves* and become *Cost of goods sold* when sold, so *Books* no longer shows a loss on paper for stock that has not been sold. Goods lost, damaged or found go to *Stock lost, damaged or gained*. The stock report, its download and the books show the same stock value. Stock that was already on the shelves (an older shop, or one moved across) is given its value once, as an opening entry; old bills are not worked out again.
- **Changed on purpose:** a purchase of stocked goods is no longer a cost on the day it is bought; it is a cost when sold (things bought that are not kept in stock are still costs when bought). The item's *Cost price* is the last price paid; it is used when there is no stock to average. Not yet: lot-by-lot cost, closing a period, a stock value on a given date, the cost on the printed bill.

### Counter PCs (the shop's other PCs connect to the main PC)
- **One main PC, many counter PCs, on the shop's own network (*Settings → Store network*, owner only; decision 4).** The owner ticks *Let counter PCs connect* and restarts the Hub once. The Hub then also listens on the shop's network with **the shop's own certificate** (made on the main PC and kept there; nothing is bought or fetched from outside), so counter PCs can open the shop's screens in a browser. Off by default: with it off, nothing is added and the Hub answers on this PC only, as before.
- **Pairing with a one-time code.** The owner presses *Make a pairing code*; it works once and runs out after ten minutes. The counter PC opens the pairing page, types the code and a name, and keeps a pass that scripts cannot read. The main PC keeps only a fingerprint of the pass. Five wrong codes lock an address for ten minutes. A page explains, step by step, how to make the counter PC trust the shop's certificate once, with check letters to compare.
- **A computer that is not paired is refused with one plain page** that says nothing about the shop, even before the licence page. This PC itself is never stopped. Forwarding headers are never believed.
- **Remove or switch off, at once.** Removing a counter PC closes its open screen and refuses its next request; switching counter PCs off refuses every computer on the network. Pairing, removal and refusals are in *Activity*.
- **The licence's number of PCs limits the counter PCs** (the main PC counts as one), with the words of the shop owner. Database step 14, with its way back. The guide is `docs/STORE-NETWORK.md`.
- Fixed on the way: with counter PCs on, the Hub could not have started (the list of allowed host names from the settings file cannot be added to); *Activity* can be opened by a link (`settings?tab=activity`).
- Not yet: what a counter PC shows when the main PC is off, a restart button, the firewall rule in setup, trust steps for tablets and phones, the key in the system's credential store. Not verified: a second real PC, a real router, Windows Firewall.

### Backups
- **Nightly copies to a second place (*Settings → Backups*, owner only; decision 11).** The owner chooses a place (a USB drive, or a folder on another PC), a time and how many copies to keep. Every night the Hub copies the whole shop there, **opens the copy and checks it**, keeps its fingerprint, and writes down every try, good or not. If the PC was off at the time, the copy is made when it is switched on. Only the newest nightly copies are kept; copies made by hand and any other file in the place are never touched.
- **An unplugged drive is told in plain words, and the till goes on.** A place is marked when it is chosen, and a copy is made only where the mark is found, so an unplugged drive (or a different one) is never mistaken for the place, and nothing is written to the PC's own disk by mistake. The first screen tells the owner when copies are switched off, when the last try did not work, or when there is no good copy for a day and a half. After a failure the Hub tries again an hour later.
- **Putting a copy back, on the same PC or on a new one.** *Put this copy back* (or, on the first screen of a PC with no shop, *I already have a copy of my shop*) checks the copy (damage, books that do not add up, made by a newer program) and sets it aside; the next time the program starts, it keeps the shop as it is now in a folder of its own, puts the copy in its place and shows what happened. Nothing is thrown away.
- **An update's safe copy goes to the same place** when the owner has chosen one. The guide for the owner is `docs/BACKUP-AND-RESTORE.md`. Not yet: copies are not encrypted, and an online copy is not built (it stays off).

### Who may do what, checked by the Hub itself
- **The permission checks now stand inside the Hub, not only in the screens.** Before, a screen hid what a person may not do, but a command called directly (by a new counter PC, an assistant, a script) was done for anybody. Now every command that changes money, stock, people or settings asks first, and the person is looked up again each time, so someone switched off, or given another role, loses their rights at once. The commands covered: building and finishing a bill, payments, returns and cancelling, discounts, stock changes, items, customers and suppliers, buying, offers and coupons, people and roles, settings, moving across from the older POS, the AI helpers, the business map and the event history, and the commands of tables and orders, the kitchen, library loans and fines, projects and bookings. A refusal is written to the audit log.
- **A command that names nobody is refused** in the shop people use. The only ways round are the program's own work (tidying overnight, the sample company), and the first set-up of a shop that has no people yet (settings and the owner only).
- **A cashier's discount limit is now held by the Hub.** The limit the owner sets (Settings) used to be checked only by the till screen; a cashier giving a discount any other way was not held to it. Now it is: a cashier may give none unless the limit is above nothing, and not more than the limit counting every discount typed in; a refused discount leaves the bill as it was.
- **Not yet covered** (listed in `docs/OPEN-WORK.md`): reading reports and the registers, the event sources, and stores other than this one.

### One sale per request
- **A sale is made once, however often it is asked for.** A bill can now carry a request key (database step 12, a unique index; the till sends one for each sale). Asking again with the same key (Pay pressed twice, or a retry after the answer was lost on the network) gives back the bill made the first time and does nothing more: no second payment, no second stock move, no second number, no second entry in the books. Without a key everything works as it did.
- **Proved under load:** 25 sales at the same moment each get their own number (no gap, no repeat) and each is whole (payment, stock, books); ten requests with the same key at the same moment make one sale; two counters selling the last unit at the same moment sell it once, and the other is told "Only 0 left" when the shop does not allow stock below zero. The transaction that writes a sale now says "wait for your turn" on purpose; the tests fail if it is changed.

### Safer updates
- **An update of a shop that has data no longer starts without a safe copy.** The Hub copies the whole shop file first and **checks the copy** (SQLite's own integrity check, the same structure number, the same tables). If the copy cannot be made or fails the check, the update does not start, nothing in the shop is changed, and the owner sees a plain page ("The update has not started") that says what to check. Before, a failed copy was only noted and the update went ahead.
- **An update is all or nothing.** All the steps run in one transaction: if one fails, the shop stays exactly as it was (before, the steps before the bad one stayed).
- **Where the copies go is a setting**, `Hub:BackupFolder` (a second disk or a USB drive is better than the same disk). Without it the copy is made next to the shop's file, as before.
- **`DatabaseCheck`** looks at a shop file without changing it (damage, records that point at nothing, and that the books add up to nothing), for a restore on a clean PC.

### Fixed
- **Goods returned from a bill that used loyalty points gave back the full price of the line.** They now give back only what was paid for them (the points' share of the line stays with the points). A test pins it.
- **A sale thrown away at the till could leave offer rows behind** that a later sale given the same number would pick up. They are removed with the sale.
- **Taking a line off a bill could break the screen that showed the bill's amounts.** Line numbers keep a gap when a line is removed, and the till, the order screen and the printed bill found each line's amounts by its number. They now find them by the line's place in the list. A test pins it.
- **Setting up with the sample company could fail with "That document was not found."** The Hub's background tidying (which clears sales left open for more than a day) could start at the very moment the sample company was being made, and take its sales, which are dated days back. The tidying now waits until the shop's set-up has finished, and the sample company marks the shop as set up only when it is fully made. Tests pin both.

### New
- **Tax registers (*GST registers*, *VAT registers* ... named after the country's tax; owners and managers).** A page with the register of bills to customers, of credit notes given and of purchases (number, date, name, the buyer's or supplier's tax number, amount before tax, each tax part, the extra tax, what else made up the total, the total), for the days chosen, each with a download for a spreadsheet. Where the country's pack names the lists its tax return is made from (India: B2B, B2CL, B2CS, credit notes to buyers with and without a GSTIN), each bill goes in the first list whose rule it meets, with the limits written in the pack and not in the program; a bill of a shop that is not registered for the tax, and a cancelled bill, is in no list. Where the pack names a code for goods (India: HSN or SAC), a summary by code and rate counts what was sold in the whole period, credit notes taken off, and says how many lines have no code. Where the pack names the blocks of the periodic return's summary (India: sales inside the state, between states to buyers with and without a GSTIN, nil, exempt; the same for purchases), a *Summary* tab adds up the **lines** of every bill, credit note (taken off) and purchase of the period into those blocks, so that a bill with a taxed line and a nil line is split, and counts any line that fits no block. A bill now keeps the buyer's (or supplier's) tax number as it was when the bill was made, so a register never changes when the customer's number does (a bill made before this step gets the number its customer has now, once). New database step 11 (one column, with a tested way back).
- **An item code and an extra tax, when the country's tax asks for them.** The country pack now says whether goods carry a code (India: an HSN or SAC code) and a further tax set per item (India: cess), and under which words. In such a country the item screen shows both fields, a bill line keeps a copy of both as they were when the line was made, the extra tax is worked out by the tax engine (taken out of the price once when prices include tax; the older program took it out twice and got the taxable value wrong), and the bill prints the code under the line and the extra tax under the country's word for it. A country whose pack names neither shows nothing and charges nothing. Goods taken back, a bill made from a quote and free goods keep the code and the extra tax of the line they came from. *Move from the older POS* now keeps the older program's code and extra tax on the item. New database step 10 (two columns, with a tested way back).
- **Offers, coupons and gift vouchers (*Offers*, for owners and managers).** The shop can make: a percent off one item; buy so many and get so many free (the till adds the free ones on a line of their own, stock moves for them and nobody pays tax on them); money off a bill whose items come to an amount between two limits (the till uses it by itself and the cashier can leave it out); a gift voucher given with a bill, to a named customer, to use on a later visit (its words are set in *Settings*); and coupons made for chosen customers, each with its own code. A customer can also be given a standing discount (*People → edit*), which goes on every line by itself and wins over an item offer. At the till a coupon or voucher code is typed in; it is used up only when the bill is made (never when it is only tried), two tills cannot use the same code, and a cancelled bill gives it back. Every offer has first and last days that are both tested against today. Nothing is sent to anyone from the Hub. The older program's fixed discount for a customer now comes across with *Move from the older POS*. New database step 9 (new tables and a few columns, with a tested way back).
- **Quotes for a shop sale.** At the till, *Keep as a quote* saves the sale being built as a numbered quote for the customer. It is worked out exactly like a bill, with the same tax and discounts, and nothing is sold, no stock moves and no money is taken. *Make a bill from this quote* (on the quote) starts a bill with the same customer, lines and prices, which comes to the same total, ready to be paid; the quote then says which bill it became and cannot be billed again. (The older program's quote showed no tax, so the bill made from it came to more.)
- **Books page (for owners and managers).** *Books* shows, for the days chosen, what came in and what went out (from the double-entry books), what the shop has, owes and is worth at the end of the last day, and every account with its debits and credits, which are always equal. Purchases count as costs on the day they are bought; the value of stock still on the shelf is not counted yet, so "left over" is not the final word on profit.
- **Loyalty points (off until the owner turns them on).** *Settings → Loyalty points*: what an item earns when it has no setting of its own (a percent of what the customer pays before tax, or points for each one sold), and what one point is worth. An item can have its own setting under *Items*. A named customer earns points when a bill is made, and can use points on a later bill as money off (before tax); the till shows the balance, the bill shows points earned and used, and the customer's account lists every line. A customer cannot use more points than they have, and a return or a cancelled bill takes the points back. Points are worked from what the line comes to after its discounts. New database step 8 (a points ledger that is never edited, two columns on bills, with a tested way back).
- **A customer's account.** *People → Account* shows what a customer owes (or has in credit) line by line, and a supplier's account shows what the shop owes. *Receive money* takes a payment without naming a bill: the oldest unpaid bills are settled first and anything over is kept as credit for the next bill. At the till, *Use credit on account* pays part or all of a bill from that credit without moving any money. Taking something back can now give the money back or keep it as credit on the customer's account (the cashier chooses). A cancelled bill that was partly paid from credit gives the credit back and refunds only the money. The credit limit now counts what the customer's account says, so money owed from before and returns kept as credit are counted. Taking the rest of a bill in the chosen way after using credit needs no extra step.
- **The shop's books, double entry (first step, no screen yet).** Every bill that becomes final, every payment and refund and every cancelled bill is now written into books where each entry adds up to nothing (the owner's decision 32). Customers and suppliers have accounts that say what they owe and are owed; money brought across from an older system is an opening entry; a return kept as credit leaves the customer with a credit on their account. The books are never edited (the database refuses it); a mistake is put right with a new entry. Bills made before this step are posted automatically the first time the shop is opened. New database step 7 (new tables only, with a tested way back). The screens for the customer account, receiving money, the trial balance and the profit and loss come next.
- **Discounts at the till.** A line can be given a discount as a percent or as an amount (type `10%` or `25`), and a whole bill can be given one too. A discount on the whole bill is spread over the lines first and the tax is worked out on what is left, so the tax falls with the discount (the owner's decision 33; the older Windows POS left the tax unchanged). The bill shows "Discount given". Goods taken back from a discounted bill are credited at what was paid for them, whole or in parts. Owners and managers may always give a discount; a cashier only up to a limit set in *Settings → Business* (none until the owner sets one). New database step 5 adds only columns and has a tested way back.
- **Move from the older POS (first step, *Settings → Move from the older POS*, owner only).** A shop that used the older Windows POS can bring across its items (with the real barcodes, the retail and wholesale price, the tax and the reorder level), its stock, its customers and suppliers, and what each of them owes. It has two separate steps. **Check** reads the older program's database (it only reads; the password is typed each time and is never saved) and shows a report: the older program's figures next to what the Business Hub would hold, with every difference explained, every row left out with its reason, and notes where a person should look. It saves nothing. **Move it** is possible only when nothing in the report blocks it and the owner has ticked that they read it: a copy of the Business Hub's data is made first, everything is saved in one go or not at all, and running it again adds nothing twice. New database step 6 (tested way back) keeps a log of moves, which old row became which item or person, and the balances owed on the day. Not moved by this step: the history of sales, purchases and payments, extra tax, batches and serial numbers. The balances are saved but no screen shows them yet.
- **Two looks: a list look for a monitor and a counter look for a touch screen.** *Settings → Look → The two looks*. The list look puts the menu along the top, shows the items as rows you scroll down and puts the bill below. The counter look has big buttons, the menu on the left, the items in the middle and the bill on the right. The owner chooses one for the shop, lets each screen decide (a touch screen gets the counter look, any other screen the list look), or gives one computer its own look. Nothing changes until the owner chooses. Only the selling screen has a different shape in the two looks so far.
- **AI helpers (optional, off by default)**: *Settings → AI helpers* for the owner. Eight switches, a description of the computer, AI services with permissions per kind of data, limits and a use record, keys kept in the system's safe, and a list of models with a way back. Needs the `ai` part in the licence. The shop's own screens do not use it and are unchanged.
- **Business event history (optional, off by default)**: *Settings → AI helpers → Business events* for the owner. What cameras and sensors saw is kept apart from what happened in the business; every event says who or what, where, when, how sure, why the system believes it and what it rests on; a wrong event is marked wrong or replaced and stays in the history. Records are forgotten when their time is up (short for what is private, biometric data not kept at all unless the owner says so, card details never), even with the switch off.
- **Business map (optional, off by default)**: *Settings → AI helpers → Business map* for the owner. The places (a shop, its areas, shelves) and devices (cameras, sensors) and how they connect to each other and to the shop's own products, people and bills. The shop's own records are only looked at, never copied; what they already say (a payment pays a bill) is worked out when asked.
- **Safer updates**: before an update changes an existing shop's database, the whole file is copied next to it (`shop.db.before-update-….bak`), and the new database step has a tested way back.

### Changed
- **The Hub carries its own copy of the language data (ICU)** and no longer runs without it. Nothing has to be installed first. This was needed so that the reader for the older program's SQL Server database can connect at all. Numbers, dates and text are still written the same way on every PC whatever language the PC is set to (the shop's country settings decide how money and dates look). The program's folder is about 30 MB bigger. The Linux package was built, installed and started with it here; the Windows side is checked by the release workflow on a Windows runner.
